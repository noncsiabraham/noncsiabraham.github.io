Lichen

For humans, the environment becomes landscape when it is approached in a contemplative relationship, in a free meditation without a practical purpose. Landscape is a metaphor of all that defies our will, it leaves us with the process of questioning our very existence. Therefore landscapes have not only aesthetic but existential significance in our lives, they are evoking emotions such as melancholy or nostalgia, homeliness or fright. The series explores this dual significance of landscape. In the process, I drew on my own archival landscapes, using a digital anomaly to create unknown spaces beyond the original images.

3D

Stone is one of the most fundamental material forms that surrounds us. Humanity has used it since ancient times as a building material, relic, and religious symbol. I attempted to create a modified version of this natural form. By entering and being processed in digital space, the object has been confronted with its original form of existence, resulting in a new object with opposite material composition and attributes. I was also interested in the visual aspect of this recreation process, so in addition to creating the object, my goal was to map the digital process using the tools of photography.

Under Construction

My understanding of spatial experiences evolved significantly when I spent a period of my life in Rotterdam. With new spaces to explore, I began a process of visual documentation, observing these environments through my daily movements. I paid close attention to the emotions these unfamiliar places evoked in me. Rotterdam’s industrial and modern architectural style and urban design stood in stark contrast to my familiar environments. This contrast led me to discover what are often referred to as non-places, spaces without a clear function or identity. To connect with them, I used a visual language that allowed me to personally observe my presence within these ambiguous environments. The chosen medium for this photo series was risograph printing, a technique primarily used in graphics. The distinctive visual qualities of risograph printing invite the viewer to question their perception of the depicted spaces. It adds a tactile, almost abstract dimension that reinforces the uncertainty of these environments.
